Rose Culture
Exposure: sunny site with good air circulation
Soil: deep and well-drained 
Water: Water thoroughly, especially during dry periods. Keep water off leaves to prevent disease. 
Feeding: Fertilize regularly during growing season 
Mulch: To retain soil moisture and as winter protection

The Dean Bond Rose Garden

The Dean Bond Rose Garden is comprised of over 650 roses, displaying over 200 different types. The roses are arranged in eight distinct beds, each highlighting specific classes of roses. Two large outer beds are primarily devoted to Species and Old Garden roses. The inner beds display Modern Bedding roses, including many examples of Hybrid Tea, Floribunda, and Grandiflora. Climbers and Ramblers are supported on the posts and chains that surround the garden.

The roses are labeled by name and class. The collection is updated on an annual basis and serves as a display of the best roses for growing in the Delaware Valley.

Photo of Isaac Clothier, Courtney Smith, John C. Wister and Robert Pyle's sister, at the dedication of The Dean Bond Rose Garden.

The Dean Bond Rose Garden commemorates Elizabeth Powell Bond, the Dean of Women at Swarthmore College from 1890 to 1906.

Established to memorialize the Dean and her love of roses, the garden was made possible through a special bequest of Robert Pyle '97. Designed by Gertrude Wister in 1956, the rose garden exhibits the great diversity of the rose genus.
The Rose Garden is part of the Scott Arboretum on the Swarthmore College campus. The Arboretum grows over 5,000 different kinds of plants, which create the college landscape and provide a display of the best ornamental plants recommended for Delaware Valley gardens.
